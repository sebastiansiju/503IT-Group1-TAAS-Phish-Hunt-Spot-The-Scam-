## 2. Day 2 Concept Development: Final Project Topic
**File Reference:** `Day-2-Concept-Development/final-topic.md`  
**Commit Message:** `Add Day 2 final topic`  
**Assigned Student:** Student 1  

### Project Title
**Phishing Awareness & Digital Resilience for Teens**

### Chosen Topic Overview
The chosen core topic focuses on **Phishing Awareness**, moving away from standard, dry corporate modules and leaning heavily into the specific social engineering threats encountered by young internet users. 

### Topic Description & Framework
Modern phishing is no longer limited to classic spam emails. For teenagers, the primary risk vectors are **Smishing** (SMS phishing), malicious hyper-targeted direct messages (DMs), account cloning, and counterfeit login portals. This project unpacks how threat actors weaponize human psychology—specifically triggering fear, curiosity, or greed—and teaches concrete verification patterns.

### Core Learning Objectives
* **Isolate Threat Vectors:** Recognize scam infrastructure across everyday social media channels (Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat) and chat environments (Discord).
* **Deconstruct Psychological Exploits:** Recognize the warning signs of engineered panic ("Action required inside 1 hour") or artificial scarcity ("You won't get this free item again").
* **Establish Verifiable Habits:** Teach students how to safely navigate suspicious environments by cross-checking sources, verifying domain URLs, and isolating account credentials with Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).

## 3. Day 2 Concept Development: Target Audience Analysis
**File Reference:** `Day-2-Concept-Development/target-audience.md`  
**Commit Message:** `Add Day 2 target audience`  
**Assigned Student:** Student 2  

### Target Demographic Profile
* **Age Group:** 14 to 16 years old (High School / Key Stage 4)
* **Digital Profile:** Mobile-first, active content consumers and creators with persistent connectivity.

### Behavioral Environment
Traditional platforms like email are rarely accessed by this target profile outside of structural education spaces. Instead, digital footprints are clustered around real-time interactive apps (Discord, Snapchat, TikTok) and persistent online multiplayer ecosystem environments (Roblox, Steam, Epic Games). 

### Vulnerabilities Unique to Ages 14-16
1. **The Ecosystem of In-Game Monetization:** Scammers lure teenagers with the promise of third-party shortcuts, zero-cost virtual currency generation (e.g., "Free Robux/V-Bucks"), or counterfeit digital items/skins.
2. **Social Proxy Exploitations:** Teenagers heavily trust network circles. Attackers routinely hijack an established peer's account to push malicious links down the trusted contact list.
3. **FOMO & Artificial Urgency:** Due to rapid emotional reactions, teens are highly prone to clicking deceptive links when told their account is facing immediate suspension or when presented with fake high-value celebrity giveaways.

### Pedagogical Strategy for Content Delivery
* **Interactive Gamification:** Replace flat informational slideshows with mock scenario challenges ("Is This Link Safe?") and responsive simulated choices.
* **Context-Driven Visual Layouts:** Use exact, high-fidelity UI simulations of standard mobile app notifications, direct messages, and specialized verification setups.
* **High-Impact Takeaways:** Emphasize immediate physical actions over dense definitions: *Stop, Check the domain URL, never copy-paste verification keys.*
